Concept Initial product concept

AI Cloud Cost Monitor

A concept for helping teams on AWS make sense of their cloud bill. It is an idea we are exploring, not a working product. This repository does not contain an AWS integration yet.

  • Understand spending

    Break a cloud bill into services, trends and drivers that are easy to read at a glance.

  • Spot unusual changes

    Surface sudden cost shifts early, with context about what changed.

  • Find optimization ideas

    Point out places where spend might be reduced, for a person to evaluate.

  • Review before acting

    AI-written explanations and recommendations are suggestions. People decide what to do.

Illustrative demo data. Every number and chart below is made up to show the idea. Nothing here is connected to an AWS account, and no savings are verified.

What we are building

Three product directions. All of them are planned, and none are shipped yet.

  • Planned

    AI-assisted cloud insights

    Plain-language explanations of what is happening in a cloud environment and why, so teams spend less time digging through consoles and bills.

  • Planned

    Developer workflow automation

    Taking repetitive operational chores off a developer's plate, with automations that stay visible and easy to review.

  • Planned

    Clear, actionable recommendations

    Suggestions that say what to change, why it matters and what the trade-offs are, so decisions are easy to make and to explain.

Our principles

How we intend to build. These describe our approach, not features or certifications that exist today.

  1. Useful over complicated

    Start with real problems developers have, and build the simplest thing that helps.

  2. Transparent by default

    Show how conclusions are reached, and be plain about what works today and what does not.

  3. Human control over automated actions

    AI can suggest and people approve. Nothing should change your systems without your say.

  4. Security and least-privilege access

    Our design goal is to ask for the minimum access a tool needs. This is a principle we are building toward, not a claim about controls that already exist.
